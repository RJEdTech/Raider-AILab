The Mirror: 95% right, for whom?

    For teachers: the math, the data and the sources

    The math. Weighted average (Statistics; Algebra 1 mixture problems): headline = share₁ × score₁ + share₂ × score₂ + …, with shares that add to 1. In the picture each column’s area is its share times its score, and the dashed headline is the level the green would settle at if poured flat.

    The data. The four scores are the error rates Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru measured for IBM’s gender classifier in “Gender Shades” (2018, Table 4): wrong 0.3% of the time for lighter-skinned men, 7.1% for lighter-skinned women, 12.0% for darker-skinned men and 34.7% for darker-skinned women, 87.9% right overall on their benchmark. The other two products tested, from Microsoft and Face++, showed the same pattern. The products sorted every face as male or female, so the study used those two labels. Their benchmark has 1,270 lawmakers from Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa, Iceland, Finland and Sweden.

    What this toy assumes. The opening test mix copies IJB-A, a U.S. government face benchmark of the time: 59.4% lighter-skinned men, 20.2% lighter-skinned women, 16.0% darker-skinned men and 4.4% darker-skinned women. The page applies the rates measured on her benchmark to other mixes of people. Real error rates also depend on the photos themselves, so the “95 right in 100” headline is a what-if, not a number IBM reported.

    Sources. Buolamwini and Gebru, “Gender Shades,” Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 81, 2018 (paper). MIT News, February 2018, for her story and the 97% claim tested on photos more than 77% male and more than 83% white (article). Raji and Buolamwini, “Actionable Auditing,” 2019, for the follow-up: IBM’s new version in 66 days, its error for darker-skinned women at 16.97% in August 2018, and its overall error down 7.7 points from 12.1% (article).

    How to use it in class. It is built to run cold. A good stopping point for discussion is step 3, after students have slid the test both ways: who should decide what goes in the test? Answers can be copied at the end and pasted into Canvas.
