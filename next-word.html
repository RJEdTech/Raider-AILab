How a language model works

Next Word: why the bot said 3:30

    For teachers: the math, the model and the sources

    The math. Conditional relative frequency (Statistics, two-way tables and conditional probability): P(next word | word) = count(word, next word) ÷ count(word). Temperature reshapes the chances: each share is raised to the power 1 ÷ T, then the shares are rescaled to add to 100%.

    What this toy is. A bigram model: it counts which word follows which in ten invented team-chat messages, and looks back exactly one word. The chat and the story are written for this tool. It never gives a chance to a word pair it has not seen; real models give every token a small chance.

    How real models differ. A real large language model uses a neural network over word pieces called tokens, reads the whole conversation at once, and learns from enormous training sets. Meta reported that Llama 3 was pretrained on more than 15 trillion tokens and chooses each next token from a vocabulary of 128,000 (Meta, “Introducing Meta Llama 3,” April 18, 2024). “About 200 screens” in the last step is 15 trillion tokens ÷ roughly 70 tokens in this chat ÷ 1 billion. Chat apps add more training so the model follows instructions, and some can search or read what you paste, but each reply is still written one token at a time.

    How to use it in class. It is built to run cold, with no introduction. A good stopping point for discussion is step 5, after three sentences: all of them sound right, and few are true. Their answers can be copied at the end and pasted into Canvas.
